> Kamarkat gives you the platform a platform team would build, without the platform team. You own the box and everything in it.

## What Kamarkat is

Kamarkat is a complete platform stack for startups that want a golden path and cannot yet staff a platform engineering team. It runs on affordable infrastructure in your own cloud account rather than on a hyperscaler's managed services, and it covers everything your applications need to run: cluster lifecycle, GitOps delivery, secrets, certificates, DNS, a gateway with login, a database, an identity provider and observability.

The foundational unit is the **box**: your management cluster and the managed clusters it manages, all running in your cloud account under credentials only you hold. The **platform services** (the GitOps engine, cluster lifecycle and the operators that reconcile everything else) run on your management cluster. There is no separate management tier above it and nothing hosted for you, so your box keeps running whether or not Kamarkat is reachable.

Kamarkat is free and open source. The stack is tested as one set, published as one version, and proposed to your repository as an upgrade you accept.

## What you can do with Kamarkat

- **Run a production platform in your own cloud account.** Your bill, your credentials, your clusters.
- **Ship applications without operating infrastructure.** Publish a chart from your application's build; the platform renders its namespace, gateway, login, database, secrets and network policy.
- **Use the platform tools your apps need.** A database, an identity provider and an observability stack come with the platform, at versions tested together. You own what you do with them.
- **Upgrade by accepting a pull request.** A new platform version arrives as a one-line change to your repository. Nothing changes until you merge it.
- **Leave without migrating.** Every artefact your box runs is mirrored into infrastructure you control, so nothing you run depends on Kamarkat staying reachable.

## How Kamarkat is organized

Everything starts with a **box**. Each box belongs to one **tenant**, and holds one management cluster plus any number of managed clusters.

A **GitOps repository**, `<tenant>-gitops`, is the single source of desired state for the box. For each cluster it records two things: the platform version that cluster runs, and the values it runs with.

The **bundle** is the platform itself: every component, at versions tested together, published as one versioned artefact.

**Capabilities** are the platform tools the bundle provides. Some are machinery every box runs; others, such as the database and observability, you select. Your **applications** run on top of them, each in its own isolated namespace.

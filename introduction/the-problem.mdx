> Building a product shouldn't require building an entire infrastructure platform first.

Every business that ships cloud-native software ends up with a platform underneath it. Usually nobody decided to build one. It accumulates, one tool at a time, and it becomes a second product that the team maintains alongside the one its customers pay for.

That second product costs you three ways.

## Integration

A complete cloud-native platform is a dozen technologies that have to agree with each other: a cluster and its networking, delivery, a secret store, certificates, DNS, a database, an identity provider, a gateway, dashboards. Each has its own configuration surface, its own dependencies and its own release cadence.

The integration is the work. Installing the components is the easy half, and the half that looks finished.

## Maintenance

The platform does not stop needing attention once it exists. Deployments, incidents, security updates, platform changes and infrastructure maintenance all compete with building the product. The competition is permanent, not a phase.

Each component also upgrades on its own schedule. The failures that cost the most happen between components, so every upgrade is a fresh integration test you run in production.

## Inconsistency

A platform assembled piece by piece has no single way to be operated. Each component brings its own interface, its own idea of identity and its own notion of what an operation is. Every engineer who touches it has to learn each one.

AI agents inherit the same problem. An agent pointed at an independently assembled platform meets inconsistent tools, conventions, permissions and workflows, and improvises across them. So a business ends up doing the same undifferentiated work twice: once to assemble its platform, and again to teach agents how to operate it.

## Kamarkat's answer

Install the platform instead of assembling it. Kamarkat ships the infrastructure and platform services already integrated, tested as one versioned set, and installed through one CLI into a cloud account you own.

- **Integration** is done before you install. A new cluster arrives with its networking, delivery and registration configured, and an application's chart gets its routing, login, database and secrets.
- **Maintenance** of the platform simplifies into coordinated version changes. Kamarkat publishes the whole platform as one tested version, and your own upgrade automation proposes it as one change for you to review. Upgrades can still need attention, and some cannot be undone by reverting them.
- **Inconsistency** is addressed by design: Kamarkat establishes a consistent operating model. Every cluster, capability and application is declared and reconciled the same way, and the agentic layer, in development, is designed to work through the same contracts.

You keep what distinguishes your business: your code, your data and your decisions. [Welcome](/introduction/welcome#from-download-to-running-platform) sets out what you provide and what the platform supplies.

## What this does not claim

Infrastructure still needs operating, and agents do not change who is accountable for it. What changes is how much of the platform you assemble, how consistent it is to operate, and whether an agent working on it is improvising against a stack nobody standardized.

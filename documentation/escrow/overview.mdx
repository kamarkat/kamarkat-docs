> Escrow is what makes a box recoverable. It is deliberately small, deliberately outside the box, and deliberately reachable by one person.

Your box runs its own secret store. That store holds everything your applications need, it is reconciled by the platform, and it lives inside the cluster — which is exactly the problem when the cluster is what you have lost. Escrow is the separate, external store that holds the few things you cannot rebuild a box without.

## Why the platform has escrow

Because the alternative is a circle. The box's own Infisical is encrypted with keys the box holds; the database behind it is restored from backups whose credential the box holds; the cluster is reached with a kubeconfig the cluster issues. Lose the cluster and every one of those answers is inside the thing that is gone.

Escrow breaks that circle by keeping a small number of artifacts somewhere the box does not control and does not host — Infisical's hosted service, in a project **you** own. Nothing in it is needed while the box is healthy. Everything in it is needed the day the box is not.

## What belongs there

Only what satisfies one test: **it must be readable when the management cluster is unavailable.** That is master keys, the identity provider's key, admin kubeconfigs, at-rest encryption keys, your workload clusters' certificate authorities, the key your management-state backups are encrypted with, and the credentials that reach your cloud account and your backup bucket.

What does **not** belong there is everything else — and that is most things. Your applications' secrets, their database credentials, the values the platform's controllers read: those live in the box's own self-hosted Infisical and arrive in Kubernetes as ordinary Secrets. Mirroring them into escrow would copy your entire secret estate to a second place for no recovery benefit. See [Artifacts and scopes](/documentation/escrow/artifacts-and-scopes) for the exact list and the reasoning per artifact.

## Who accesses it

One configured human — the **Platform Administrator** — authenticating with `kakt login`, plus the machine identities your automation needs. The box declares which human it accepts, by Infisical's own account id.

Two things are worth being precise about from the start, because they are easy to conflate: `kakt` refusing an undeclared identity is a **guard rail**, and **membership of the escrow project** is the authorization boundary. Infisical enforces the second. [Platform administrator and access](/documentation/escrow/platform-administrator) explains both and where the line between them falls.

## How a box reaches its escrow

```text
   your repository                 Infisical (hosted)              your cloud account
   ──────────────────              ──────────────────              ──────────────────
   registry/clusters/<c>/
     values.yaml
       escrow.url          ───────▶  the escrow project
       escrow.projectId              /hub-operator/<boxId>/
       escrow.organizationId           provider-credential-hetzner ──▶ servers, LBs,
       platformAdminIdentity           backup-store-credential          volumes, networks
                                       zitadel-masterkey
   kakt login  ──────────────────▶     infisical-master-keys
   (your own session)                  clusters/<cluster>/
                                         admin-kubeconfig
                                         secret-encryption-key
```

The coordinates are committed to your repository because they are **names, not credentials** — a url and a project id grant nothing, and keeping them only on one laptop is what used to make a second workstation unable to operate the box. The credentials come from your own authenticated session.

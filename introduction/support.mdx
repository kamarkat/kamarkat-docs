> The software is free. What you pay for is someone keeping it maintained, which is the platform engineer you would otherwise hire.

## What maintenance covers

Maintenance means every capability your box runs is **available, current, recoverable and correct as shipped**. Operators keep reconciling, versions stay supported, backups are taken and restorable, failover works, storage can grow and components are patched.

Maintenance does not cover what you do with a capability. A slow query against a healthy database, or a misconfigured login client registered in a healthy identity provider, is yours. When a capability is working as shipped, zero-ops tells you so and helps you, but does not own the outcome.

| | zero-ops maintains | You own |
| :- | :- | :- |
| **Database** | The engine, replicas, backups, failover | Schemas, queries, migrations, data |
| **Identity** | The identity provider and its availability | Your users and the clients you register |
| **Gateway** | Routing, TLS, login | Which routes and hosts your apps declare |
| **Applications** | The platform they run on | Their code, images and configuration |

## The maintenance promise

zero-ops **never forces an upgrade**. You can always decline a proposal and your box keeps running.

Each published version is supported for a window stated when it is published. A version then moves through four stages:

| Stage | What it means |
| :- | :- |
| **Supported** | Full maintenance promise |
| **Deprecated** | Still maintained, with an end date announced |
| **Unsupported** | Best-effort answers, no undertaking about outcome |
| **End-of-life** | No undertaking at all |

None of these stages stops a cluster running. What lapses is the obligation, not the software.

**Security fixes are proposed straight away** rather than waiting for the next upgrade. Each proposal states what it fixes and what declining it leaves exposed.

## Support is based on evidence

zero-ops cannot see into your box, and nothing it runs connects into one. Support is based on what your box **exports**:

- The health and reconciliation state of the control plane
- The platform version and the component versions it resolved to
- Drift, policy violations and the outcome of upgrades

Workload data, application logs, database contents, secrets and end-user identifiers are **never exported** for support.

Collection is done by the **Support Agent**, an open-source component shipped to every cluster. It does nothing until you enrol it, it sends only an allowlisted set of control-plane facts, and you can read what it would send before it sends anything.

<Note>
A maintenance claim extends exactly as far as the telemetry you export. A box that exports nothing still receives every version and every proposal, but carries no undertaking about the outcome on its clusters.
</Note>

## What a box costs

You pay your cloud provider directly for the infrastructure your box runs on. The target for a production-capable minimum box is **€150–400 per month**. This target has not been measured yet, and zero-ops will not cut replicas to meet it: a capability that needs high availability to be supportable ships highly available.

## Frequently asked questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="What happens if I stop paying for maintenance?">
    Nothing stops running. You keep your control plane, your clusters and a mirrored copy of every artefact they run. Proposals stop arriving and the version you run eventually leaves support.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can I run zero-ops without a subscription?">
    Yes. The platform is open source, and the control plane you would run is the same one zero-ops tests and publishes, not a reduced edition.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Does zero-ops have access to my clusters?">
    No. zero-ops holds no cloud, cluster or secret credential of yours. Its authority ends at publishing a version; your own automation proposes it and your own control plane applies it.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

> zero-ops may propose changes to your platform. It is never the authority that makes them take effect.

## What it is

A box is everything zero-ops gives you: a **management cluster** that runs your control plane, and the **workload clusters** it creates and reconciles. It runs in your cloud account, under credentials you hold, and it belongs to exactly one tenant.

The control plane is **delivered, not hosted**. It is the same control plane zero-ops tests and publishes, not a reduced edition. Once it is running it reconciles your clusters from your own repository and depends on nothing zero-ops operates.

## What zero-ops never holds

| Credential | Where it lives |
| :- | :- |
| Cloud provider credentials | Your control plane |
| Cluster admin credentials | Your box, with a break-glass copy escrowed in your own cloud account |
| Secret material | Your box's secret store |
| Write access to your repository | Your own automation |

Because no zero-ops component serves more than one tenant, a compromise of zero-ops cannot reach your running infrastructure. Isolation between customers comes from the topology itself: there is no shared control plane to break into.

## How a change reaches your box

1. zero-ops **publishes** a new platform version.
2. Your repository's own dependency-update automation **proposes** it as a pull request that changes one version field.
3. A **pre-flight check** runs inside your box against the target cluster and attaches its verdict to the proposal.
4. You **merge** it, or configure your repository to merge qualifying proposals automatically.
5. Your control plane **reconciles** the new version.

zero-ops does step 1 and nothing else. Every change reaches running infrastructure through your repository and your control plane.

## Reaching your box

You sign in to your box as a person, through your box's identity provider. The cluster admin credential is a **break-glass artefact**: it is escrowed outside the box, in your own cloud account, and used only when ordinary access has failed.

## What you need to know

### Your box keeps running without zero-ops

If zero-ops is unreachable, your box keeps reconciling. If you stop your subscription, nothing is withdrawn, because nothing was ever held on your behalf.

### zero-ops cannot see inside your box

Support depends on what your box exports. See [Support and Maintenance](/introduction/support) for what is exported and what never is.

### Every box carries a full control plane

There is no shared control plane between tenants. That is what makes the custody claim true, and it sets the floor on what a box costs.

## Frequently asked questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Can zero-ops push a change to my clusters?">
    No. zero-ops holds no access to your repository or your clusters. A change takes effect only when your repository says so and your control plane applies it.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What if a published version is defective?">
    A published version is never changed in place. zero-ops publishes a new version that supersedes it, and that version reaches you as a proposal like any other.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

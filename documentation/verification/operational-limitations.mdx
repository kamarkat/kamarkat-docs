> Seven details that shape how you use verification. Each one is a boundary of what an assessment establishes.

## A subset establishes only what it ran

A run restricted to named checks runs those and no others. Its verdict says `selected validation passed — complete cluster acceptance not assessed`, and that wording is load-bearing. The run cannot accept the cluster, cannot replace a full assessment's evidence, and cannot move your box's verdict. A behaviour nobody executed stays unknown no matter how green the line looks.

## Explaining asserts nothing

Asking the command to explain itself reads your declarations and reports what a full assessment would do. It probes no surface, creates nothing, and changes nothing. It also never reports that anything passed, because it ran no check.

## Acceptance creates and removes short-lived resources

Asserting a behaviour sometimes requires exercising it. An assessment may request a certificate it then deletes, or ask for a secret to be delivered and remove it afterwards. Those resources are uniquely named, carry nothing sensitive, and are cleaned up by the run that made them.

What an assessment never does is alter production material. It does not rotate a signing key, replace a certificate your workloads use, write to a database, repair a controller, or reset any part of a build. If a check cannot assert a behaviour without mutating something you depend on, it reports `unverified` instead.

## A report is evidence, not a watch

An assessment describes one cluster, on one platform version, at one moment. Nothing updates it afterwards. A cluster accepted yesterday can break today through a change outside the platform, such as an expired credential at a provider or a DNS record edited by hand. Continuous observation is what your observability capability and convergence checks are for.

## A pending managed cluster is not covered

Your box's verdict reads `incomplete` while any cluster you declared is still being built. The management cluster's acceptance is real and its evidence is complete; the pending cluster has no evidence at all. Nothing in an `incomplete` verdict says anything about the cluster that is pending, and treating `incomplete` as "almost accepted" is the one misreading to avoid.

## Day-0 takes longer, and fails more often

A run that asserts behaviour spends minutes probing real surfaces, where a run that only reconciles finishes sooner. Runs also fail that previously reported success over an unasserted box. Both are intended. A failed assessment keeps everything the run built and everything it committed, so correcting the defect and assessing again does not rebuild your infrastructure.

## Report retention is yours to set

An assessment run in your CI publishes its report as a run artifact and a job summary, and your CI keeps it for as long as you have configured. The platform does not hold a copy anywhere outside your infrastructure. A report you need months from now is one you archive on purpose.

> An assessment is evidence. It names the cluster, the version, the moment, and every check that did not run.

A verdict on its own is not evidence. A report is what lets you answer, a week later, whether a cluster was accepted, on which platform version, and what nobody checked.

## What a report records

| Field | Why it is there |
|---|---|
| The cluster assessed, and whether it is your management cluster or a managed one | a result belongs to one cluster |
| The management cluster that owns it, for a managed cluster | the assessment reached it through that cluster |
| The platform version and the committed revision assessed | a verdict about one version says nothing about the next |
| When the assessment ran, and its run identity | evidence is a point in time, not a state |
| The convergence result | acceptance follows convergence, and the two are reported separately |
| Every check: its scope, whether it applied, and its result | so a pass can be distinguished from an absence |
| The counts: applied, passed, failed, pending, unverified, inapplicable | the shape of the assessment at a glance |
| The reason for every omission | an omission without a reason is indistinguishable from a pass |
| The verdict | one line, for the cluster and for your box |

## Where it lands

An assessment run by your Day-0 pipeline publishes its report through your CI: as a run artifact, and as a summary on the job. An assessment you run on your workstation writes it beside the run.

Your CI holds it for as long as you configure it to. That retention is yours to set, and it is finite, so a report you intend to keep is one you archive deliberately.

## Reading an omission

Three results mean a check did not establish anything, and they mean different things.

**`inapplicable`** means your declarations give the check no subject. A check for the database capability on a box that declares no database is inapplicable, and the report names the declaration that makes it so. This is the only omission that is expected.

**`pending`** belongs to a cluster, not a check. You declared the cluster, and it is not yet assessable. The report names which of the three [pending reasons](/documentation/verification/cluster-states) applies. Your box's verdict is `incomplete` while any declared cluster is pending.

**`unverified`** means the check applies and could not run. A credential was missing, its subject could not be reached, or a prerequisite never arrived. This is not a pass, and a cluster with an unverified required check is not accepted. Treat it exactly as you would a failure: something is unestablished that should have been.

## What a report does not tell you

A report describes the cluster at the moment it was assessed, on the version it was assessed against. It is not a health signal and does not update itself. A cluster accepted on Tuesday can be broken on Wednesday by a change neither you nor the platform made, which is what convergence checks and your observability are for.

Treat a report the way you treat a test run: evidence that something held once, against a named version, with its omissions written down.

## Using it in a release decision

Two questions a report answers that a pipeline status does not:

1. **Was every behaviour you depend on asserted?** Read the applied count against the inapplicable list. A long inapplicable list on a box with many capabilities enabled is worth a second look.
2. **Was anything left unestablished?** Any `unverified` count above zero means a required behaviour was not checked, whatever the verdict line says.

A full assessment with zero failures, zero unverified checks and an inapplicable list you recognise is the evidence worth acting on.
